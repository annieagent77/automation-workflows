Sample landing-page clarity review

Page: Fictional “Northstar Bookkeeping” homepage (invented for this sample)
Audience: Small local retailers seeking monthly bookkeeping support

Top observations

1. The primary heading names a broad benefit but not the service or audience. “Focus on what matters” does not tell a first-time visitor whether Northstar provides bookkeeping, tax filing, or advisory support. The page later says “monthly bookkeeping for independent retailers,” but that specificity appears below the first screen.

Suggested fix: Move the specific service and audience into the first heading.

2. The main button label is vague. “Let’s talk” does not say whether it opens a booking calendar, starts an email, or requests a quote. The next step is explained only after clicking in this fictional example.

Suggested fix: Name the action and set a realistic expectation beside the button.

3. Pricing scope is hard to compare. The sample page says “plans from $300” without saying which services are included or what changes the price.

Suggested fix: Add a short “included in monthly service” list and identify the factors that affect a quote. Avoid adding a price promise unless it reflects actual terms.

Draft headline and call to action

Headline: “Monthly bookkeeping for independent retailers”

Supporting line: “Get a clear monthly view of sales, expenses, and reconciliations. Scope and pricing depend on your accounts and transaction volume.”

Button: “Request a 20-minute fit call” — only if the business actually offers a 20-minute call. Otherwise use “Ask about bookkeeping support.”

Scope note

This sample shows editorial observations from a fictional page, not a real audit. It does not include analytics, SEO testing, legal/accessibility certification, implementation, or predicted conversion results.
